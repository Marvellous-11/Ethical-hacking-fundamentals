# Ethical Hacking Fundamentals

## Day 1 – Introduction to Ethical Hacking

## Overview

Ethical hacking is the practice of identifying, testing, and reporting security weaknesses in computer systems, networks, and applications with the owner's permission. The objective is to improve security by discovering vulnerabilities before malicious attackers can exploit them.

This documentation summarizes my Day 1 learning from an Ethical Hacking Bootcamp and serves as part of my cybersecurity learning journey.

---

# What is Hacking?

Hacking is the process of discovering and exploiting weaknesses in computer systems, networks, or applications. While the term is often associated with cybercrime, hacking itself is not inherently illegal. Its legality depends on whether authorization has been obtained.

---

# What is Ethical Hacking?

Ethical hacking is the authorized process of identifying security vulnerabilities so they can be fixed before attackers exploit them.

Unlike malicious hackers, ethical hackers work with the permission of the system owner and follow legal and professional guidelines throughout the assessment.

### Objectives of Ethical Hacking

* Identify security vulnerabilities.
* Improve an organization's security posture.
* Prevent unauthorized access.
* Protect sensitive information.
* Reduce the risk of cyber attacks.

---

# Types of Hackers

## White Hat Hacker

White Hat hackers are authorized security professionals who legally assess and improve the security of systems. They work as penetration testers, security analysts, or cybersecurity consultants.

**Key Characteristics**

* Authorized
* Ethical
* Legal
* Defensive security

---

## Black Hat Hacker

Black Hat hackers gain unauthorized access to systems for malicious purposes such as stealing information, financial fraud, deploying malware, or disrupting services.

**Key Characteristics**

* Unauthorized
* Illegal
* Malicious intent

---

## Gray Hat Hacker

Gray Hat hackers operate between White Hat and Black Hat hackers. They may access systems without permission but do not always intend to cause harm. However, unauthorized access remains illegal regardless of intention.

---

# Ethical and Legal Responsibilities

An ethical hacker must:

* Obtain written authorization before testing.
* Respect the agreed Rules of Engagement.
* Maintain confidentiality.
* Protect client data.
* Report vulnerabilities honestly and responsibly.
* Avoid causing unnecessary damage to systems.

---

# Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA)

A Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA) is a legal contract that requires ethical hackers to keep all information obtained during an assessment confidential.

---

# Penetration Testing

Penetration testing is a controlled security assessment in which authorized professionals simulate real-world attacks to identify vulnerabilities before malicious attackers can exploit them.

---

# Security Frameworks Introduced

## OWASP (Open Worldwide Application Security Project)

OWASP is an open-source community focused on improving the security of software applications.

It provides resources such as:

* OWASP Top 10
* Web Security Testing Guide (WSTG)
* Mobile Application Security Testing Guide (MASTG)
* API Security Top 10

OWASP primarily focuses on securing web applications, mobile applications, APIs, and software systems rather than physical security.

---

## NIST (National Institute of Standards and Technology)

NIST develops cybersecurity standards, frameworks, and best practices that help organizations identify, protect, detect, respond to, and recover from cybersecurity threats.

Unlike OWASP, NIST provides broader guidance that includes people, processes, technology, and organizational security.

---

## PTES (Penetration Testing Execution Standard)

PTES provides a structured methodology for conducting professional penetration tests.

Typical phases include:

1. Pre-engagement interactions
2. Intelligence gathering
3. Threat modeling
4. Vulnerability analysis
5. Exploitation
6. Post-exploitation
7. Reporting

---

# Key Takeaways

* Hacking is the process of discovering and exploiting vulnerabilities.
* Ethical hacking always requires authorization.
* White Hat hackers protect systems.
* Black Hat hackers exploit systems illegally.
* Gray Hat hackers may have good intentions but still operate without authorization.
* Confidentiality is a fundamental responsibility of ethical hackers.
* Penetration testing is a controlled simulation of cyber attacks.
* OWASP focuses on application security.
* NIST provides comprehensive cybersecurity guidance.
* PTES defines the standard penetration testing methodology.
